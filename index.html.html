Hangul Alphabet

한글 자모 & Pronunciation

한글 — The Korean Alphabet

Hangul (한글) was created in 1443 by King Sejong. It has 14 consonants (자음) and 10 vowels (모음). Most people can learn to read it in just 2–7 days! Each syllable block combines consonants + vowels together.

🔊 About Double (Tensed) Consonants

Double consonants (쌍자음) are pronounced with extra tension — like holding your breath and then releasing the sound with more force. They sound "harder" than regular consonants.

🔊 About Compound Vowels

Compound vowels (복합모음) are combinations of basic vowels. Many sound like quick blends — for example, 와 = ㅗ + ㅏ = "wa". With practice, they become natural.

📦 How Syllable Blocks Work

In Korean, letters are grouped into syllable blocks. Each block has at least one consonant + one vowel.

C + V + C한ㅎ + ㅏ + ㄴhan

🧩 The Silent ㅇ Rule

When a vowel starts a syllable, you must write a silent ㅇ before it. For example: 아 (a), 이 (i), 우 (u). When ㅇ appears at the END of a syllable block, it sounds like "ng" — like in "sing".

📚 Reading Direction

Korean is read left to right, top to bottom — same as English. Within a syllable block, consonants on top come before the vowel, and bottom consonants (받침, batchim) come last. Example: 학 = ㅎ (top left) + ㅏ (right) + ㄱ (bottom).

🇳🇵

Nepali Speaker Advantage

Many Korean consonants have similar sounds to Nepali: ㄴ≈न, ㅁ≈म, ㄷ≈द, ㄹ≈र/ल, ㅂ≈ब, ㅅ≈स, ㅎ≈ह. This gives you a big head start!

🔊

3 Types of Consonants

Plain: ㄱ ㄴ ㄷ ㄹ ㅁ ㅂ ㅅ ㅇ ㅈ ㅎ
Aspirated (breathy): ㅋ ㅌ ㅍ ㅊ
Tensed (hard): ㄲ ㄸ ㅃ ㅆ ㅉ

🎵

Vowel Tips

Basic vowels: ㅏ(a) ㅓ(eo) ㅗ(o) ㅜ(u) ㅡ(eu) ㅣ(i). The hardest is ㅡ — it's a sound between "u" and "o" with lips spread flat. Practice: say "ew" but don't round your lips.

📝

Writing Stroke Order

Korean strokes follow these rules: left to right, top to bottom. For syllable blocks: write the consonant first, then the vowel. Consistent stroke order helps with speed and legibility.

🔄

Consonant Changes

ㄹ sounds like "r" between vowels (바라) but like "l" at end or before consonants (발). ㅎ can become silent or blend with next consonant: 좋아요→조아요 (sounds like).

Your Korean Learning Roadmap

As a beginner (especially with a Nepali-speaking background), here is the most efficient path to learning Korean — step by step.

📍 Step-by-Step Roadmap

1
Week 1–2

Master Hangul (한글) — The Korean Alphabet

This is the MOST IMPORTANT first step. Hangul has 14 consonants and 10 vowels. It was scientifically designed and is very logical — most people can learn it in 2–7 days. Good news: Some Nepali sounds (like ㄷ=द, ㅁ=म, ㄴ=न) are very similar! Use the Alphabets section in this app to practice.

2
Week 2–4

Learn Basic Pronunciation & Sounds

Korean has sounds that don't exist in Nepali or English: ㅡ (between "o" and "u"), tensed consonants (ㄲ,ㄸ,ㅃ,ㅆ,ㅉ), and aspirated consonants (ㅋ,ㅌ,ㅍ,ㅊ). Focus on vowel accuracy — it matters a lot in Korean.

3
Month 1–2

Build Core Vocabulary (This App!)

Learn 1000+ most common Korean words — which is exactly what this app is for! Focus on: greetings, numbers, days, family, food, and basic verbs. Use flashcards daily for 15–20 min. Aim for 10 new words per day.

4
Month 2–3

Learn Basic Grammar Structure

Korean grammar is SOV — just like Nepali! (나는 밥을 먹어 = I rice eat). Learn: sentence particles (-은/는, -이/가), politeness levels, basic verb conjugation. Use TTMIK (Talk to Me in Korean) — it's free and excellent.

5
Month 3–5

Immersion — Watch Korean Content

Start watching Korean dramas with Korean subtitles. Try "Reply 1988," "Crash Landing on You," or "My Mister." Use HelloTalk/Tandem to chat with native speakers. Listen to K-pop and try to understand lyrics.

6
Month 5+

Practice Speaking & Writing

Start speaking from day one. Find Korean language partners (iTalki, Preply). Practice writing sentences daily. Consider TOPIK (Test of Proficiency in Korean) to measure your progress.

🇳🇵

Nepali Speaker Advantages

Korean and Nepali share SOV sentence structure, similar politeness levels, and some overlapping sounds. Your Devanagari reading skills help you pick up pronunciation patterns faster.

⏰

Daily Study Plan

Morning: 10 min flashcards
Afternoon: 10 min grammar (TTMIK)
Evening: 20 min Korean drama
Total: 40 min/day = conversational in 1 year.

🧠

Memory Tricks

Use mnemonics: 사과 (apple) sounds like "sa-gwa." Korean months are number + 월: 1월=January, 2월=February. Very logical! Numbers follow clear patterns too.

🎵

Learn Through K-Pop

Artists like BTS, BLACKPINK, and IU use everyday Korean. Songs repeat common phrases — great for listening practice. Try looking up lyrics as you listen.

📱

Best Free Resources

TTMIK (talktomeinkorean.com), Duolingo Korean, Naver Dictionary (best Korean dictionary), HiNative for pronunciation feedback, Anki for spaced repetition flashcards.

🎯

TOPIK Goals

TOPIK 1 (Levels 1–2) = Beginner survival Korean. TOPIK 2 (Levels 3–6) = Advanced. Aim for TOPIK 1 after 6 months of study. It's recognized worldwide for jobs in Korea.
